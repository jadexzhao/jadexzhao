02 / How I think

Curiosity came first

I have always liked technology because I enjoy understanding how things work. That habit is older than any job title. It is why I ended up in Informatics. Software, accessibility, and human-centred AI were ways to keep asking the same question with better tools.

I grew up in a family Chinese restaurant in central Indiana (Greenfield, Indiana). A page either survives a Tuesday dinner rush or it does not. During the pandemic I built the digital side so people could see the menu and order online. That was an early, practical test. It is not the only reason I care about this field.

At IU's Laurie Burns McRobbie Serve IT Clinic I moved from intern to Website Team Lead. The work is websites, WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) checks, documentation, and handoffs staff can keep running when the student team leaves. Digital Humans research asks the same thing of interfaces that talk back: can a person see what the system is, and get a human when it should stop?

I publish coding notes and handoff guides on matchaxmoxie for first-generation and low-income students finding their way into Informatics.
