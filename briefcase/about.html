I have always been curious about how technology works. I like taking a complicated idea apart, trying a small build, and explaining it in language I would have wanted earlier. That habit is older than any job title.

Over time it became software I could hand to someone else, accessibility work at IU's Laurie Burns McRobbie Serve IT Clinic, and student research on Digital Humans ... useful business interfaces when AI (artificial intelligence) communicates like a person. I grew from a web development, AI and accessibility intern into Technical Project Lead. The work is websites, WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) checks, documentation, and handoffs staff can keep running when the student team leaves.

The first system that had to survive a Tuesday was my family's restaurant. During the pandemic I built the digital side so online ordering was not a rumour. That is still a useful test. It is not the only reason I care about this field.

I am a first-generation college student. That is part of why I publish coding notes and handoff guides on matchaxmoxie for first-generation and low-income students finding their way into Informatics.

Off the clock I drink matcha, keep plants, take pictures, walk, and read. A duck farm is a long-term creative throughline, not a product I am selling. Those habits are why this site looks like a garden journal. The case studies are why a hiring manager should stay.
