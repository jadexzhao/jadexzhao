Digital Humans

I'm an Informatics student at Luddy and the student project lead for Digital Humans. The question is product-sized: if an AI learns your vulnerabilities, goals, and communication style, then meets other people's AIs before you do, what earns trust? I am building the research before I claim the answer.

The provocative version is an AI concierge that does the first round of dating for you. The product question comes before the product: what does it know, what does it disclose, and who gets the final say?

The work

Digital Humans are not just avatars. They are interfaces that borrow the social cues of a person. That can make a system easier to approach and easier to misunderstand.

One proposed future is an AI concierge that talks with other people's concierges, filters the noise, and brings you only a few human matches to meet. It sounds efficient. It is also intimate, consequential, and easy to get wrong.

Before asking whether people will adopt it, I ask what the system can infer, what people can see, what they can consent to, and who is accountable when it fails.

In HCI (human-computer interaction), disclosure cannot be a footnote. It has to match what the system actually does, at the moment a person needs to decide.

When the interface becomes a proxy

In a 2024 Bloomberg Technology Summit conversation, Whitney Wolfe Herd described a dating concierge that could learn what makes a person vulnerable, talk with other people's AI concierges, and return only a few humans worth meeting. The promise is less burnout. The uncomfortable part is that the system would handle the most personal context before two people ever meet.

  • Promise: reduce the endless scrolling and small talk that make online dating feel like unpaid labor.
  • Risk: turn insecurities into data, preferences into rankings, and a recommendation into a decision that feels inevitable.
  • Design test: disclose what the system inferred, show why it made a recommendation, and let the person correct, refuse, or leave the process.

That is why the idea drew both excitement and a Black Mirror comparison. A useful concierge can reduce friction. A persuasive one can quietly become the relationship.

Read the coverage: CNBC, Fortune, NBC News, and Futurism.

The digital-human hypothesis

This is my interpretation, not a claim about Wolfe Herd's private beliefs: if an AI concierge is meant to handle intimate context, a digital human could become the interface that makes that disclosure feel possible. That same realism could also make manipulation harder to notice.

1. Trust before the match

A sterile text box may be a poor place to share a breakup, a fear, or a commitment issue. A face, voice, and responsive expression could lower the barrier to honesty. It could also invite people to trust a system before they understand what it records, infers, or remembers.

2. A buffer, not a replacement

The strongest version of the concierge idea is not that AI replaces romance. It is that AI absorbs the repetitive search, awkward small talk, and rejection before a person chooses to meet another person. The gate has to stay human: the system can surface a possibility, but it cannot decide that someone is worth your life.

3. From swiping to conversation

Swiping turned a person into a card. A conversational digital human could make the interface feel more alive, but "more alive" is not the same as more truthful. The next interface shift needs a visible boundary between empathy as an experience and empathy as a claim.

The research test: can a digital human make vulnerable interaction easier while keeping the system's limits, incentives, and final decision visible?

NSF research

Earlier NSF-related research work gave me the first version of this question: can people understand what a system is asking of them and still choose to use it? Digital Humans is where I keep pressure-testing that question.

PIT-UN

PIT-UN (Public Interest Technology University Network) gave me a go-to-market lens: a useful idea still has to reach people, explain itself, and survive contact with real use. Serve-AI was founded via PIT-UN, separate from Serve IT.

Undergraduate research with IU Undergraduate Education
