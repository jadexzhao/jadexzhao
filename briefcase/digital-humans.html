Digital Humans

I'm an Informatics student at Luddy and the student project lead for Digital Humans. The product question is not whether AI can seem human. It is whether AI can help people show up more honestly without replacing the human relationship.

Public, high-level summary only ... not peer-reviewed lab claims.

The work

Digital Humans are not just avatars. They are interfaces that borrow the social cues of a person. That can make a system easier to approach and easier to misunderstand.

Before asking whether people will adopt a system, I ask what it can infer, what people can see, what they can consent to, and who is accountable when it fails.

In HCI (human-computer interaction), disclosure has to match what the system actually does, at the moment a person needs to decide.

Design tests I use

  • Can a person inspect and correct the profile being used?
  • Can they delete it or refuse at each stage?
  • Does the system reduce friction without hiding what it is?
