portfolio note — localization & register

the same message, said two ways

translating content for a uk audience isn't a spelling swap. it's a shift in directness, formality, and what gets left unsaid. this breaks down where the two overlap, where they diverge, and where one register simply has no equivalent.

language & formatting

mechanical differences
spelling
us color, favorite, organize, center
uk colour, favourite, organise, centre
dates
us month/day/year — 08/14/2026
uk day/month/year — 14/08/2026
currency
us $
uk £
sign-off
us kind regards — works either side
uk yours sincerely (named) / yours faithfully (unnamed) — uk-specific, signals fluency

register, by tone

where it actually diverges

business casual

structurally close to the us version. the uk still leans a little more indirect, even here.

"just wanted to check" — not "let me know"

a touch more small talk before the actual ask.

professional

this is where it splits hardest. directness gets replaced with understatement, the real uk signature move.

"there may be one or two points worth clarifying before friday"

same meaning as "the scope is unclear," said smaller than it is on purpose.

the piece that doesn't translate

iu gear has no uk equivalent

american college spirit-wear culture, the crewneck, the game-day identity, doesn't map onto uk universities at all. there's no register to translate it into, because the thing itself doesn't exist there.

two honest options: drop it and show casual vs. professional only, or keep it and reframe the caption as the point itself, something like "this format doesn't map onto uk conventions, and knowing that is part of the skill." for a uk-facing portfolio, that second option is the stronger move. it shows fluency in three tones, and awareness of where a us-specific register simply has no counterpart at all.
