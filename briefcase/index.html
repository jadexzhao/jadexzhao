The major

What Informatics is

Informatics, in the way the Luddy School uses it, is the study of how people, information, and technology fit together. It is not just coding, and it is not just computers. It is building and judging information systems for real people and organisations: who uses them, whether they can actually use them, and whether the organisation can keep them.

That is why Serve IT belongs in an Informatics capstone. The clinic is the course where that idea meets a real partner, a nonprofit or a town, and the work has to survive the handoff.

The clinic

Laurie Burns McRobbie Serve IT Clinic

Serve IT is an undergraduate tech clinic at the Luddy School, Indiana University Bloomington, and a way through the Informatics capstone. Community-engaged learning: students earn course credit with local nonprofits and government. People come back for more semesters, pick up leadership, and leave with work that had a real partner.

The path is INFO I389, then INFO I489. I389 comes first: 3 credits inside the clinic. I489 is the capstone, 6 credits over two semesters. The clinic asks for a minimum of 3 semesters in total. To enrol in I489 you need those 3 credits of I389, good standing, the clinic application, and the core courses finished. INFO I300 or I308 can still be open, but only if that course is taken alongside the first three credits of I489.

The hard-to-teach parts are the point: client communication, project management, and handing work off so staff can keep it.

The clinic runs specialised teams. Mine has been the website side and the accessibility side.

  • Website teams design or rebuild in WordPress, with mobile layout and accessibility in the brief, then hand the site to staff.
  • Digital Accessibility and Usability teams assess sites and write recommendations. Student materials follow WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines), with WebAIM and WAVE in the teaching set.
  • Other desks exist: cybersecurity (NIST-based audits), business analysis and data visualisation (Tableau), and Teach IT workshops. I have not done that work.

Serve AI is a separate project on the clinic site, funded through PIT-UN (Public Interest Technology University Network). It is not this ladder.

I started in Jan 2024, as a freshman accessibility tester, and I am still in it. Each semester was a different partner. The list below is one ladder, not five jobs.

  1. Jan 2024 to May 2024

    Accessibility tester, Serve IT Clinic

    The bottom of the ladder. A freshman start. WCAG 2.1 as a habit: keyboard and screen reader, then written recommendations. This is not a second Nashville.

  2. Aug 2024 to Dec 2024

    WordPress redesign lead, Middle Way House

    Middle Way House is a Bloomington nonprofit that supports people affected by domestic violence, sexual assault, and human trafficking. Rebuild with staff and a student team. Restructured journeys so essential information is findable. Built for handoff.

  3. Jan 2025 to May 2025

    Web development lead, Indiana Phenology

    Indiana Phenology is a nonprofit for citizen science on seasonal change in nature. A public science resource. Navigation for public visitors and for research users.

  4. Aug 2025 to Dec 2025

    Web designer, Greater Bloomington Chamber of Commerce

    The Greater Bloomington Chamber of Commerce supports local businesses in Bloomington. Pages with staff, for organisations without a developer on call. Navigation, structure, accessibility. Stakeholder asks became page decisions.

  5. Aug 2026 to present

    Accessibility auditor, Town of Nashville

    The Town of Nashville site is the municipal site for Nashville, Brown County, Indiana: council, utilities, police, and town services. Assessment of townofnashville.org. Recommendations only. No live-site edits from the student team. Keyboard and screen-reader paths, then a prioritised list the town can act on after the clinic leaves. Nashville case study.
