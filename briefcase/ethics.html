Ethics of human-centred AI

When an AI (artificial intelligence) system looks and communicates like a person, the ethics question is not whether it can seem human. It is whether people can see what it is doing, refuse it, and get a human when it fails.

Public, high-level summary only ... not a published law and not peer-reviewed research.

What I hold the work to

I use the same tests on Digital Humans work, clinic builds, and writing. A warm interface is not evidence of understanding, and a demo is not a finished system.

  • Can a person inspect and correct the profile or memory being used?
  • Can they delete it or refuse at each stage?
  • Is the system disclosed before the first answer, not in a footnote?
  • Is there a clean path back to a human when the system should stop talking?
