Designing for access

A website is not useful if people cannot navigate it. This page is about method, not unpublished partner scores.

Overview

Serve IT is Indiana University's student technology clinic. I started as an intern and now lead the website team: intake, scoping, delegation, and delivery. Accessibility sits in that loop, not as a stamp at the end.

Problem

Clinic partners often have no developer on call after the demo. A finding that says "improve accessibility" does not help them. A finding that names the page, the barrier, and a fix someone else can try does.

My role

Website Team Lead and, for Town of Nashville in Fall 2026, team lead / co-lead on an accessibility assessment. I write process and handoff. I do not publish client issue lists here.

Approach

  • Use the page the way a keyboard-only visitor would.
  • Check headings, labels, link text, and contrast on real content.
  • Write the finding so a teammate or staff member can reproduce it.
  • Leave a handoff, not a performance.

What a reviewer can inspect

  • Town of Nashville process page ... existing assignment write-up. That HTML is not edited from the Work redesign.
  • Middle Way House ... named because it is already on the public clinic showcase. Fuller case after share approval.
  • Civic inventory on Jade practice.

Limitations

This is not a WCAG certificate and not a list of partner defects. I am not inventing pass rates or before-and-after traffic. Methods are public. Findings stay with the partner.
