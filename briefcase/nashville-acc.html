06

Reflection

Mid-semester reflection that still reads complete for hiring: what I know now, what I am still building, and what I refuse to overclaim.

What I learned

Leading an ACC is different from shipping a website build. The product is clarity: scope, evidence, prioritisation, and language a town office can keep. Co-leading with Aanya taught me that rhythm (Planner, Thursday meetings, Canvas alternation) is part of the deliverable, not admin noise.

Challenges encountered

Holding the recommendation-only boundary when the instinct is to “just fix it.” Translating WCAG-minded findings into staff-actionable steps without dumping jargon. Keeping private clinic detail off a public portfolio while still showing real process.

What I would improve

Earlier public-safe artifacts (homepage context figure, standards matrix excerpt) so graders and hiring managers can see the work without waiting for final handoff. Tighter written severity rubric before the first full evaluation pass.

Team communication lessons

Write the ask before the meeting. Put owners on Planner cards. Alternate Canvas admin so co-leads both stay fluent. Route client outreach through clinic supervision ... do not freestyle town contacts.

Ethical considerations

Accessibility is access and civil rights framing, not a compliance checkbox for its own sake. Recommendations-only ethics: we do not silently change a live municipal site; we leave staff with honest priorities. No overclaiming finished audit results that do not exist yet. No publishing vulnerability detail that could harm the town.

Technical growth

Moving from “I can build accessible pages” toward “I can lead an assessment pipeline”: tool triage (WAVE, axe, keyboard), evidence notes, and handoff writing. That growth compounds for Digital Humans, product, and internal-systems work too ... same instinct: will this hold for the people who have to live with it?
