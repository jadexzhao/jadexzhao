Overview

Problem statement

Residents and visitors use the Town of Nashville website for public information and services. Accessibility barriers shut people out of content they have a right to use. ServeIT tasked our team with an ACC (accessibility assessment) of https://www.townofnashville.org/: evaluate the public site against WCAG-minded practice, then deliver recommendations the town can act on. This is assessment and handoff, not a redesign sprint on the live domain.

02

Client

Client organisation: Town of Nashville (Brown County, Indiana). Public website: https://www.townofnashville.org/. Town-side contact is coordinated through ServeIT supervision. Private emails stay off this portfolio.

What the client wanted

An accessibility assessment of the public town website, with findings packaged so staff can improve access for residents and visitors ... without relying on a student team to edit the live site. Scope of work: inspect, test, and document; compile a report; present; hand off. Do not change the live site.

The challenge

Municipal sites are public infrastructure. When accessibility fails, people lose access to town information and services. The challenge is not “make it look newer.” It is to surface real barriers, prioritise what staff can fix and maintain, and keep the recommendation-only boundary honest.

What we are doing

Stakeholder research and standards framing · ACC training · weekly Thursday meetings · Planner backlog and sprint planning · planned automated + manual accessibility checks · prioritised recommendations document for handoff. Detail in Process.

06

Reflection

Mid-semester reflection that still has to read complete for Canvas: what I know now, what I am still building, and what I refuse to overclaim.

What I learned

Leading an ACC is different from shipping a website build. The product is clarity: scope, evidence, prioritisation, and language a town office can keep. Co-leading with Aanya taught me that rhythm (Planner, Thursday meetings, Canvas alternation) is part of the deliverable, not admin noise.

Challenges encountered

Holding the recommendation-only boundary when the instinct is to “just fix it.” Translating WCAG-minded findings into staff-actionable steps without dumping jargon. Keeping private clinic detail off a public portfolio while still showing real process.

What I would improve

Earlier public-safe artifacts (homepage context figure, standards matrix excerpt) so graders can see the work without waiting for final handoff. Tighter written severity language before the first full evaluation pass. [placeholder: one concrete improvement after the next sprint retro]

Team communication lessons

Write the ask before the meeting. Put owners on Planner cards. Alternate Canvas admin so co-leads both stay fluent. Route client outreach through clinic supervision ... do not freestyle town contacts.

Ethical considerations

Accessibility is access, not a compliance checkbox for its own sake. Recommendations-only ethics: we do not silently change a live municipal site; we leave staff with honest priorities. No overclaiming finished audit results that do not exist yet. No publishing vulnerability detail that could harm the town.

Technical growth

Moving from “I can build accessible pages” toward “I can lead an assessment pipeline”: tool triage (WAVE, axe, keyboard), evidence notes, and handoff writing. Same instinct as longer Digital Humans work: will this hold for the people who have to live with it on an ordinary day?
