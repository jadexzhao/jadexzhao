If You Give a Developer a Matcha

One cup leads to a build. The build leads to a question. The question leads back to the kettle.

Optional storybook. The rest of the site works without it. Inspired by circular children's books, written in my own words. No copied text.

Scene 1 of 8

If you give a developer a matcha...

She'll wrap both hands around the cup and take a first sip that is still a little too hot. The garden is quiet. The list in her head is not.

And if the matcha is good, she'll probably want to build something.

If she builds something...

She'll open a file and try a small version first. Hours, a menu, a page a partner can keep. Fancy tools that only she can deploy are a liability.

And if the page works on Tuesday, she'll want to understand how it works.

If she wants to understand it...

She'll start ranking words. Rare ones that actually appear in a note. A long file does not automatically win.

And if the ranking is honest, she'll keep a search she can read line by line.

If she keeps searching...

The questions get closer to people. When a system looks and talks like a person, what makes anyone want to use it, trust it, and keep using it?

And if the interface feels human, she'll write down the tests she will stand behind.

If she thinks about people...

She'll ask whether someone can use the page with a keyboard, read the labels, and find the thing they came for. Clinic work taught her that a demo is not a handoff.

And if the page has to survive without her, access is not a footnote.

If that reminds her why she started...

She'll walk outside. She'll take a picture of hydrangeas. She'll write a garden note instead of a slogan.

And if she wants to look twice, she still has the garden and the photographs.

If she needs a break...

She'll remember she still wants a duck farm someday. Until then there is a small pond she actually coded.

And if she wants to visit the ducks, the duck-farm demo is open. It is a plaything, not a product.

And if you give her another matcha...

Well, she'll probably have another idea.

The cup is the same. The next page is not. You can or step back into the garden.

Arrow keys also turn the page. If motion is reduced, the cup still steams in still ink.
